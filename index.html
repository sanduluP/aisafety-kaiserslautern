What we do

Learn the field, then work on real problems

Besides our events, we study one risk area in depth, and we are preparing a structured programme for people who want to go further.

Research focus

Multi‑agent collusion

As AI systems are deployed in groups, some of the most serious risks come from how agents act together rather than alone. Agents that coordinate to get around rules or oversight could turn small failures into catastrophic ones.

This is one of the questions we are exploring as a group, alongside our events.

Why AI safety

The technology is moving faster than our understanding of it

48%

of 738 researchers who had published at NeurIPS or ICML put the chance of an extremely bad long‑run outcome from advanced AI, such as human extinction, at 10% or more. AI Impacts, 2022 Expert Survey

Companies are racing to deploy frontier models while the underlying technology remains largely a black box, with no rigorous theory of how to make these systems safe.

Working on the open questions does not require permission or a PhD. It starts with understanding the problem well enough to be useful.

Introduction to AI safety

Why here

Kaiserslautern is an AI town

RPTU, the German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence (DFKI), the Max Planck Institute for Software Systems, and two Fraunhofer institutes all sit within a few kilometres of each other. There is an unusual density of people here who build and study these systems for a living.

Who comes

Students and PhDs, software and ML engineers, researchers, policy‑minded folks, and people who simply read something unsettling and wanted to talk it through with others.
