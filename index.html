AI Safety
Kaiserslautern

Conversations, events, and community for people who want advanced AI to go well.

We are a local group for anyone in and around Kaiserslautern who wants to think carefully about advanced AI systems — alignment, governance, evaluations, and real‑world impact. We host reading groups, talks, and informal meetups for experienced practitioners and complete newcomers alike.

What we do

Focused, local, and open to new people

Structured discussion of key papers and essays — alignment, interpretability, evaluations, and AI governance. We read the thing together, then argue about it properly.

Talks & workshops

Short talks, open Q&A, and hands‑on sessions with researchers and practitioners, for people who want to build a sharper model of the field and where it is heading.

Community

A place to meet thoughtful people in Kaiserslautern who care about making advanced AI go well — and to find collaborators, mentors, and next steps in the field.

Why AI safety

The technology is moving faster than our understanding of it

AI capabilities are advancing rapidly and hold enormous potential for good. But in a large survey of published AI researchers, 48% put the risk of an extremely bad outcome from advanced AI — such as human extinction — at 10% or more (Grace et al., 2022). Companies are racing to deploy frontier models while the underlying technology remains largely a black box, with no rigorous theory of how to make these systems safe.

We believe there are still fundamental open questions — technical and institutional — about how to ensure advanced AI is beneficial rather than harmful. Working on them does not require permission or a PhD. It starts with understanding the problem well enough to be useful.

Introduction to AI safety

Why here

Kaiserslautern is an AI town

RPTU, the German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence (DFKI), the Max Planck Institute for Software Systems, and two Fraunhofer institutes all sit within a few kilometres of each other. There is an unusual density of people here who build and study these systems for a living.

Who comes

Students and PhDs, software and ML engineers, researchers, policy‑minded folks, and people who simply read something unsettling and wanted to talk it through with others.
